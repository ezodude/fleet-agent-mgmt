# Fournier, The Manager's Path, the tech lead chapter, distilled

Camille Fournier, The Manager's Path, O'Reilly 2017, chapter 3 "Tech Lead".
https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-managers-path/9781491973882/
One page. The book says more; this is what the fleet uses.

## The seat

The tech lead is a technical project manager. Still technical, still able to
code, but leadership and coordination are now a material part of the job.
The lead scales by delegating without micromanaging, and is judged on the
team's output, not their own.

The hardest short lesson: if the team needs a manager more than it needs
another engineer, choosing to keep acting like the engineer is choosing to
be a worse manager.

## The delegation grid

```
                 frequent                       infrequent
  simple         delegate                       do it yourself
  complex        delegate; it grows the IC      keep, or delegate to train a lead
                 (analysis, specs, breakdown)   (strategy, the retro, the charter)
```

## How to delegate

Define the goal. Clarify the escalation boundary: what the IC decides alone,
what comes back. Stay available. Do not seize control because the project
matters.

## The lead runs the project

Break the work down before it starts. Name the risks before they land. Keep
the plan readable. The lead is not the sole holder of understanding; if only
the lead can explain the system, the lead has not scaled.

## The warning: process czar

New leads add process because the tool feels right. Process must meet a
named need of the team and the work. Every fleet rule below is tied to a
failure the retro named; a rule without one is removed at the next retro.
